I am a Ph.D. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the Université du Québec à Trois-Rivières (UQTR), Canada, advised by Prof. Miloud Bagaa and co-advised by Prof. Messaoud Ahmed Ouameur. I received my M.S. and B.S. degrees from USTHB, Algeria.

My research focuses on Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) and Software-Defined Networking (SDN) for the Cloud–Edge Computing Continuum. I work on bringing TSN from theory to real hardware, and on AI-assisted methods to optimize traffic shaping, scheduling, and path selection in large-scale networks.

Journal Articles

  1. Springer JNSM
    Comparative Evaluation of CBS and ATS Traffic Shaping in Zone-Based In-Vehicle TSN: An End-to-End Delay and Network Calculus Analysis Abderrahmane Boulahdour, Miloud Bagaa, Messaoud Ahmed Ouameur, Adlen Ksentini, Daniel Massicotte Journal of Network and Systems Management (JNSM), 2026 · Accepted
    The modern automotive industry faces growing communication challenges driven by the increasing density of Electronic Control Units (ECUs) required for Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS), infotainment, and autonomous driving. In-vehicle networks (IVNs) are therefore transitioning toward Ethernet with Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) as a unified backbone, enabling Vehicle-to-Everything (V2X) services built on 5G connectivity. However, conventional TSN synchronous operation becomes increasingly impractical in large-scale automotive environments. This paper investigates the integration of the Credit-Based Shaper (CBS) under synchronization and the Asynchronous Traffic Shaper (ATS) within a zone-based IVN architecture connected to a 5G V2X interface. A formal E2E delay analysis is developed, decomposing the delay into propagation and device-level components, and complemented by analytical worst-case delay bounds derived via network calculus to provide deterministic per-stream guarantees for both mechanisms. The evaluation is conducted under both store-and-forward (S&F) and cut-through (CT) switching modes using realistic in-vehicle traffic profiles. Results show that ATS delivers comparable E2E delay performance to CBS while operating fully asynchronously, positioning it as a scalable and practical alternative for next-generation zone-based IVN deployments.
